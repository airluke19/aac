If your watch already uploads runs to **Strava**, it's the quickest way to use AAC: **one tap**, no keys to copy. Your runs arrive on their own, every 15 minutes.

<Steps>

1. **Open your profile**

   In AAC tap your avatar at the top: the first section is *Where your data comes from*.

2. **Tap "Connect with Strava"**

   Strava opens. Sign in if needed.

3. **Keep "View data about your activities" ticked and tap Authorize**

   It's the only permission needed: AAC reads your activities, never writes or posts anything.

4. **Done**

   You're back in AAC: within minutes your runs from the last 12 months arrive, with map, heart rate, pace, cadence and elevation.

</Steps>

## Strava, Intervals.icu or both?

| | Strava | Intervals.icu |
|---|---|---|
| Connection | one tap | Athlete ID and API key |
| Runs with map, heart rate and pace | ✓ | ✓ |
| Resting HR, HRV, sleep | — | ✓ |
| Session adapted every morning | from load and form only | ✓ full |
| Heart-rate-reserve zones | — | ✓ |

**The coach's advice:** if you can, connect **both**. Strava brings your runs effortlessly, Intervals.icu the recovery data that lets the coach adapt every session. If the same run arrives from both sources, AAC keeps it **only once**.

## Good to know

- **Load** for Strava runs (Strava doesn't provide it) is estimated by AAC from heart rate: that's how form, load and injuries keep working.
- **Disconnecting** is always possible from your profile: AAC revokes access and deletes the runs that came from Strava.
- **Runs imported into Intervals from Strava** don't reach AAC because of Strava's terms: connecting Strava directly solves it.

<Aside type="note" title="Self-hosting">
On an instance you host yourself, the Strava option appears only after you create a Strava app: see [Configuration](/en/self-hosting/configuration/#strava).
</Aside>
