Every AAC session has a **heart rate to respect**. It isn't a number from a table: it comes from your data, and the session always tells you where it comes from ("Why this HR").

## The five zones

| Zone | % | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Z1 | 50–60% | recovery and warm-up |
| Z2 | 60–70% | easy aerobic: easy runs and long runs |
| Z3 | 70–80% | steady aerobic: steady pace |
| Z4 | 80–90% | threshold: quality sessions |
| Z5 | 90–100% | maximal |

## Heart-rate reserve: zones that are truly yours

With at least **5 resting-HR readings in the last 28 days**, AAC computes your zones on your **heart-rate reserve** (Karvonen method), the same approach used by Garmin, Polar and serious coaches:

> **Zone HR = resting HR + % × (max HR − resting HR)**

So two people of the same age but with different fitness get different zones. The fitter one has a lower resting HR and a wider margin.

**A real example.** With max HR 184 and resting HR 45, easy running goes up to **142 bpm**. With the old calculation, 70% of max HR alone, the limit would have been 128: a fit runner would have had to walk when they could jog comfortably.

## The safeguards

- **Easy running never goes above 80% of max HR**, whatever your resting HR.
- **Run-walk is more cautious**: 65% of the reserve, because that's where the base is built.
- **Resting HR is robust**: 28-day median, only values between 40 and 90 bpm. One bad night doesn't move your zones.
- **Without resting data** the standard zones as % of max HR are used.
- The **Norwegian 4×4** stays at 85–95% of max HR, as in the original protocol; **race zones** are specific to your goal.

## Max heart rate

If you know it (from a test or a race), enter it in your profile. Otherwise AAC estimates it with the **Tanaka formula: 208 − 0.7 × age**, more accurate than the classic 220 − age. The estimate can be off by ±10 bpm: that's why **your body counts too**.

## The talk test

The number is a guide, your body is the judge. **If you can speak in full sentences you're in the easy zone**, even if your watch shows a few beats more. If you're out of breath, slow down or walk.

## When they change

Zones update on their own when your resting HR or max HR changes. Every change shows up in the coach's history and, with notifications on, on your phone: *"Heart-rate zones updated: easy running up to 142 bpm…"*.
