Most running injuries come from **building up too fast**: in volume, or in a single run. AAC watches three signals, with declared thresholds.

## 1. Recent vs usual load (ACWR)

The ratio between the load of the last 7 days and that of the last 6 weeks (Gabbett, 2016).

- **0.8–1.3**: safe build-up
- **above 1.3**: caution, you'll be told
- **above 1.5**: too fast, **your next session is lightened automatically**

## 2. Weekly kilometres

A warning if the last 7 days exceed the average of the previous 4 weeks by **more than 30%**, and by at least 5 km.

## 3. The long-run jump

A study of over 5,000 runners (Frandsen et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2025) found that injury risk rises when a single run exceeds the longest of the previous month by **more than 10%**. AAC:

- warns you when it happens (over 30% is considered high);
- tells you **how many km your next long run can reach**;
- **adds up runs on the same day**: if your watch stops and records two pieces, they count as one outing.

## Where you see it

On **Today** an orange or red warning appears only when needed. In **Progress** you find the load bar, the week's km and the next long-run limit. Every warning also lands in the coach's history.

AAC is not a doctor: **if you feel pain, not just fatigue, stop.**
